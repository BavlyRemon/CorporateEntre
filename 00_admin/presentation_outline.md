## Slide 1 — Hook (≈25 s)

**One-sentence frame:** the way an incumbent firm *talks* about innovation tracks closely with what its industry lets it do — but inside each industry, language shifts reveal the strategic turns that financials only confirm later.

What the audience should leave the slide knowing:
- We read 28 CEO/stakeholder letters, 7 per firm (1997–2025).
- We measured how often each firm uses language from the **Innovation Process Model** themes and from the **explore vs. exploit** paired lenses.
- We then look at how those numbers move across leaders, eras, and industry cycles.

(Scope/sample sentence is one line — it's the required brief, not the story.)

---

## Slide 2 — Scope: four firms, two industries (≈30 s)

A 2×2 design: **tech vs. energy** for the industry contrast, with one **B2C / B2B** pair on the tech side and one **European / American** pair on the energy side. Any pattern we report has to survive both boundaries — between industries and within each.

- **Amazon (1997–2025):** B2C platform; CEO transition (Bezos → Jassy) included on purpose so we can read the same firm with two different authors.
- **NVIDIA (2017–2025):** B2B platform; the cleanest technology-discontinuity case in the corpus (pre- and post-AI-boom).
- **Shell (2014–2025):** European supermajor; stakeholder-governance model, embedded transition narrative.
- **Chevron (2013–2024):** American supermajor; shareholder-primacy model, operational-excellence + capital-discipline narrative.

This slide is brief — project scope is a constraint, not the story. The work is what we do with the set.

## Slide 3 — Framework: five themes + the words that dominated them (≈45 s)

The **Innovation Process Model** gives us five lenses; we add one cross-cutting paired lens that runs over the top. For each, the words that actually dominated our corpus:

- **Strategic Leadership** — how the CEO frames their role (founder vs. operator, decisive vs. disciplined). Dominant: *process, safety, efficient, disciplined, responsible, productive, trust*.
- **Horizon Scanning / Sense-making** — how the firm names the future (discontinuities, technologies, threats). Dominant: *AI, learn, future, demand, digital, cloud, opportunity, long-term*.
- **Purpose, Vision & Governance** — who the firm says it exists for. Dominant: *customer, experience, leader, scale, shareholder, return, dividend*.
- **Strategic Options & Experimentation** — how the firm spends choices. Dominant: *invest, develop, technology, invent, capital, partner, iterate*.
- **Agile Execution & Organization** — how the firm gets things done. Dominant: *people, fast, capability, infrastructure, process, speed, organization*.
- **Cross-cutting · Explore vs. Exploit** — paired lens over the top.
  - *Explore*: learn, invent, innovate, research, reinvent, iterate.
  - *Exploit*: cost, scale, return, safety, dividend, efficient.

This is the framework the rest of the deck operates inside.

## Slide 4 — Methodology (≈40 s)

**What we did to the text.** For each of the five IPM themes and each cross-cutting pair (explore/exploit, customer/shareholder, long-/short-term, entrepreneurial/managerial, internal/external innovation, risk/performance), we built a curated keyword list — the dictionary that defines the construct.

Three deliberate choices, each with a reason:

- **Stemming.** A word counts whether it appears as `innovate`, `innovating`, `innovation`, or `innovative`. Without stemming we'd undercount tech firms in particular — NVIDIA 2017 originally scored 0% explore share purely because Bezos-style "we are inventing" became "we are innovating" and the matcher missed the suffix.
- **Synonyms / aliases.** Each entry lists semantically equivalent surface forms (`founder` / `founders` / `founded`, `acquire` / `acquisition`, `partner` / `partnership`) and excludes false neighbours (`decisive` does **not** count `decision`; `experiment` does **not** count `experience`; `productive` does **not** count `production`). We didn't want a stemming artifact to do the analysis for us.
- **Normalization per 1,000 words.** Letters differ from 777 words (Shell 2015) to 5,251 words (Amazon 2022) — almost 7×. Raw counts would just measure how talkative a CEO is. Per-1,000-word rates measure *emphasis*: how much of the available narrative the firm spends on each construct.

The dictionary itself is the methodology. Everything else is just counting.

## Slide 5 — Tech vs. Oil: the headline contrast (≈70 s)

The four firms split along industry lines on every paired lens *except* time horizon.

| Lens | Amazon | NVIDIA | Shell | Chevron |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explore share | 0.68 | 0.65 | **0.11** | 0.27 |
| Customer share (vs. shareholder) | **0.93** | 0.83 | 0.38 | 0.30 |
| Long-term share | 0.87 | 0.87 | **0.94** | 0.91 |
| Strategic Options / 1k | 8.6 | 8.8 | 8.0 | 8.7 |
| Horizon Scanning / 1k | 7.1 | **27.0** | 3.8 | 5.7 |
| Agile Execution / 1k | 5.5 | 10.0 | 6.4 | **10.9** |

Conclusions to deliver in sequence:

1. **Industry trumps firm-level idiosyncrasy.** Two tech firms cluster, two energy firms cluster, on five of six lenses. Whatever else we say tonight, the largest variance in CEO language is *industry*.
2. **Long-term is universal — short-term is the differentiator.** Every firm talks long-term. The split shows up in *who* the long term is for: tech writes about customers and platforms; energy writes about shareholders and disciplined returns.
3. **Horizon Scanning is the tech moat.** NVIDIA spends ~4× as much narrative naming technological inflections as the energy firms do. Amazon sits in the middle. Energy firms scan less because their horizons are dictated by commodity cycles and regulation, not technology discontinuities.
4. **Agile Execution is the *only* IPM theme where energy beats tech.** Chevron at 10.9/1k narrates the most operational-excellence language of any firm in the corpus. Capital-intensive operations make execution the differentiating competence.
5. **Strategic Options is convergent (~8.5/1k for all four).** Everyone talks about choices, allocation, R&D, partnerships at roughly the same intensity — they just choose different options. This is where the IPM framework's universality holds up best.

## Slide 6 — Amazon: Bezos → early-Jassy → late-Jassy (≈70 s)

Amazon's explore-share by year tells a three-phase story:

| Year | Explore share | Era | Strategic posture (in IPM terms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1997 | 0.54 | Bezos founding | Day-1 / founder mentality (Strategic Leadership) + universe-of-options framing |
| 2016 | **0.94** | Peak Bezos | "We are inventors" — Strategic Options dominant, AWS + Echo + Studios all named |
| 2020 | 0.50 | Bezos pandemic | Mixed: explore narrative gets balanced by operational/safety language (Agile Execution) |
| 2021 | **0.80** | Bezos farewell letter | "Differentiation is survival" — record 69 explore terms; widest-platform framing of his tenure |
| 2022 | 0.60 | **Jassy phase 1** | Drop in explore intensity, surge in cost / discipline language (Strategic Leadership: managerial control) |
| 2024 | 0.76 | **Jassy phase 2** | Re-exploration through AI: Anthropic, Bedrock, custom silicon named as Strategic Options |
| 2025 | 0.60 | Jassy continuing | AI scaling — explore language moderates as bets get committed |

Conclusions:

1. **Founder vs. operator dialect is visible.** Bezos peaks twice (2016, 2021) at >0.80 explore share; Jassy's first letter drops to 0.60 — same firm, different vocabulary. The change is faster than any financial metric reveals.
2. **The 2022 "Jassy phase 1" is a textbook exploit pivot.** It coincides with the warehouse rationalization and 27,000 layoffs — and the lexical data caught the shift in the *letter* before the headcount drop showed up in 10-K filings.
3. **Jassy phase 2 (2024–25) revives Strategic Options around AI**, but never re-reaches Bezos's 2021 peak. Re-exploration is real but bounded by post-2022 capital discipline.
4. **Purpose, Vision, and Governance carries the constant.** Across founders, pandemics, and CEO transitions, Amazon's Customer-obsession / Day-1 vocabulary persists at ~13/1k. That's the durable strategic identity the IPM "Purpose, Vision, Governance" theme is meant to capture.

## Slide 7 — NVIDIA: before vs. after the AI boom (≈70 s)

NVIDIA's letters split cleanly into a **pre-AI-boom explorer** phase and a **post-AI-boom exploiter** phase, with ChatGPT (Nov 2022) as the hinge.

| Year | Explore share | Datacenter revenue ($) | Lexical posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 0.82 | 0.83B | "GPU computing has arrived" — narrating the discontinuity |
| 2018 | 0.67 | 2.9B | Naming AI / autonomous / cloud as separate Horizon platforms |
| 2020 | 0.68 | 6.7B | Mellanox + datacenter scale; Strategic Options expand |
| 2021 | **0.86** | 10.6B | Peak exploratory: explore=38, exploit=6 — every emerging compute paradigm named |
| 2022 | 0.65 | 15.0B | Brief moderation (crypto winter, gaming slump) |
| 2023 | 0.72 | 47.5B | Post-ChatGPT — narrating the AI moment with new vocabulary |
| **2025** | **0.36** | **>100B** | **First exploitative year** — AI is no longer a frontier bet, it's infrastructure being scaled |

Conclusions:

1. **Horizon Scanning is NVIDIA's signature theme** (27/1k vs. 4–7/1k for the others). Across nine years they continually re-narrate inflections — Pascal, CUDA, deep learning, Mellanox, Omniverse, generative AI, AI factories.
2. **2025 is the flip.** For the first and only time, NVIDIA's letter is exploitation-leaning. The vocabulary shifts from "we see what's coming" to "we are scaling what arrived" — execution, deployment, infrastructure, scale.
3. **The flip matches the financials.** Datacenter went from <$1B (2017) to >$100B (FY2025). At that scale, what used to be a "selective bet" (IPM: Strategic Options) becomes the operating system of the business (IPM: Agile Execution and Organization).
4. **Pattern recognition for the room:** the canonical innovation-process arc is *explorer → exploiter* once the discontinuity matures. NVIDIA is the corpus's clearest case of this transition happening in real time, in language, in a single year.

## Slide 8 — Shell vs. Chevron: European vs. American (≈70 s)

Both energy firms are exploit-leaning, but they exploit differently. Shell talks like a European stakeholder firm; Chevron talks like an American shareholder firm.

| Lens | Shell | Chevron |
|---|---|---|
| Explore share | 0.11 | 0.27 |
| Strategic Leadership / 1k | **6.1** | 3.0 |
| Purpose, Vision, Governance / 1k | 10.3 | 10.8 |
| Agile Execution / 1k | 6.4 | **10.9** |
| Risk vs. performance share | 0.15 | 0.25 |
| "Lower-carbon" / transition vocabulary | persistent (every year) | episodic (peaks 2021, fades after) |

Conclusions:

1. **Shell's signature is *discipline-as-virtue*.** Strategic Leadership at 6.1/1k — twice Chevron's — driven by `discipline`, `disciplined`, `decisive`, `responsible`, `stewardship`. The CEO is positioned as a steward managing through legitimacy concerns, not a deal-maker chasing returns.
2. **Chevron's signature is *operational excellence*.** Agile Execution dominates at 10.9/1k — `efficient`, `reliable`, `safety`, `process` — and the CEO is positioned as an operator running a returns-machine.
3. **The lower-carbon narrative diverges sharply.** Shell *embeds* energy-transition vocabulary into every letter from 2014 onward — modest in absolute terms but persistent. Chevron *flirts* with lower-carbon language: it peaks in 2021 (explore share 0.45, the only ambidextrous Chevron year) when Wirth launches the Chevron New Energies business, then **recedes** through 2023–24 as returns and capital discipline take back the narrative.
4. **Both rely on Purpose, Vision, and Governance disproportionately.** ~10/1k each. In tech, "Purpose" means Day-1 / customer obsession. In energy, "Purpose" means social license + transition framing — same IPM theme, very different content, which the slide should foreground.
5. **The "American shareholder vs. European stakeholder" reading holds up lexically.** Chevron's customer-share (0.30) is the lowest in the corpus — its addressee is the shareholder. Shell's customer-share (0.38) is higher, and it routinely names communities, regulators, and "energy progress" — a wider stakeholder set. This is the cross-cultural finding the data supports.

## Slide 9 — Cross-cutting patterns & surprises (≈55 s)

A few things the lexical data shows that survive across all four firms:

1. **Long-term is everyone's vocabulary; short-term is nobody's.** Long-term share sits at 0.87–0.94 for every firm. No incumbent uses CEO-letter air time to talk about quarters — which means "long-term orientation" doesn't differentiate strategies. *What* the long term is *for* does.
2. **Ambidexterity is rarely 50/50, and ambidextrous years are usually transitional.** Of 28 letters, only 4 sit in the 0.40–0.60 "mixed" band (Amazon 1997 founding, Amazon 2020 pandemic, Amazon 2022 Jassy pivot, Chevron 2021 New Energies launch). Each of those is a *moment of redirection*, not a steady state. The IPM framework's ambidexterity construct should probably be read as a *dynamic*, not a *position*.
3. **Horizon Scanning intensity tracks revenue growth, not size.** NVIDIA scans the hardest and grows the fastest. Shell scans the least and grows the slowest. Causation is unprovable from this corpus, but the language signal precedes the financial signal in our two cleanest cases (Amazon 2022, NVIDIA 2025).
4. **Customer-share is the cleanest industry separator we found** (tech 0.83–0.93 vs. energy 0.30–0.38). It survives the obvious counter-hypothesis that the gap is just B2C vs. B2B — NVIDIA is B2B and still 0.83.
5. **Strategic Leadership language is *thinner* than expected in tech.** Amazon 3.4/1k, NVIDIA 1.4/1k vs. Shell 6.1/1k. Tech CEOs let products and markets carry the strategic-leadership message; energy CEOs make leadership itself part of the narrative.

## Slide 10 — Conclusions, limitations, Q&A bait (≈35 s)

Three lines we want to leave the room with:

1. **Industry context dictates the vocabulary of strategy more than firm size, founder, or age.** Same five IPM themes everywhere; very different content.
2. **Inside an industry, the lexical signal moves before the financial signal.** Amazon 2022 caught Jassy's exploit pivot. NVIDIA 2025 caught the AI-as-infrastructure flip. Tracking language gives an earlier read than tracking 10-Ks.
3. **Ambidexterity is transitional, not stable.** Every "mixed" year in our corpus was a year the firm was changing direction.

Limitations we should name:
- CEO letters are crafted communications — intended narrative, not necessarily practice.
- The dictionary is opinionated; we made it auditable but it embeds our judgment.
- N=4 firms × 7 letters — patterns, not causal effects.

Open questions to invite the room into:
- Is NVIDIA 2025's exploit flip a turning point or a one-year artifact?
- Would Shell's bounded-exploration language become unbounded under a different CEO, or is that capped by European governance norms?
- Where would TSMC / Aramco / Microsoft / BP fall on the same lenses?

## Q&A prep — anticipated questions

1. **"Why these companies?"** — Project scope (two-by-two design across industries we wanted to compare). Move on quickly.
2. **"Why CEO letters and not earnings calls / 10-Ks?"** — Highest-altitude strategic narrative the firm publishes voluntarily, same author/role across years, comparable across decades. (Same scope answer.)
3. **"Aren't keywords noisy?"** — Yes. That's why we paired every quantitative finding with direct quotes and a financial anchor. The dictionary is also visible and editable, so disagreement becomes specific, not abstract.
4. **"How robust is the stem-aware dictionary vs. a basic word list?"** — Demonstrable: without stemming NVIDIA 2017 scored 0% explore share. With curated synonyms + excludes, the noise from collisions like `decisive`/`decision` and `experiment`/`experience` is removed.
5. **"Where does this differ from off-the-shelf sentiment tools?"** — Sentiment is positive/negative; we're measuring *which strategic construct the CEO is emphasizing*. Different goal.
6. **"Customer-share — isn't that just B2C?"** — Partly, yes. But NVIDIA is B2B and still 0.83. The pattern is industry-deeper than the channel.
7. **"Is N=4 enough?"** — We describe patterns, not causal effects. The framework + dictionary are designed to be re-applied to any new firm.
8. **"Is 2025 NVIDIA actually exploitative or just a writer-change effect?"** — Same author (Jensen Huang) across every NVIDIA letter in the corpus. So the language shift is intra-author, which is the strongest test we can run inside this design.
