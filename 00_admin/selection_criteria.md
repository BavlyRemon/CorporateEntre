# Selection Criteria

Each final company set must contain exactly seven report-years/letters. Years are selected to maximize analytical value, not to impose a mechanical interval.

Criteria:

1. Span meaningful strategic eras.
2. Capture inflection points in leadership, market disruption, strategic renewal, technology transition, crisis, or portfolio logic.
3. Provide substantive text suitable for close reading and lexical analysis.
4. Support comparison across two broad industries: technology/digital platforms and oil & gas/energy.
5. Preserve source integrity by using official company reports, official company letter pages, or SEC filings when official company PDFs are blocked.
6. Avoid over-weighting famous years if the available strategic text is too thin.
7. Document non-selection of plausible years so the choice is auditable.

## Archival Boundary Rule

Older annual reports can be useful, but the seven-letter close-reading set is still not intended to be a complete corporate history. Going too far back can reduce comparability because reporting formats, corporate structures, leadership regimes, and industry problems change substantially. Older years should be considered when they provide a necessary baseline or inflection point, but they should not be selected merely because they are older.

For the energy companies, the selected years intentionally privilege a contemporary strategic-renewal window: portfolio renewal, integration, energy-transition pressure, crisis response, capital discipline, acquisition options, and mature execution under investor/regulatory scrutiny. Older years are retained as robustness or background candidates when they would shift the analysis toward merger history or pre-current-cycle macro conditions.

## Devil's-Advocate Defense

- Shell could go back to 2005 for Royal Dutch/Shell unification or to 2010 for a post-financial-crisis energy-cycle baseline. Those years are historically meaningful, but they would move the paper toward governance and corporate-history analysis rather than the current CE focus on energy-transition renewal, portfolio choice, and ambidexterity.
- Shell 2014 is now included because the seven-letter design has enough room for a clean pre-BG baseline before the 2015 acquisition and 2016 integration years.
- Chevron could go back to the Texaco merger period, the Unocal acquisition, or the 2008/2010 financial-crisis energy cycle. Those are useful archival comparators, but they are less comparable to Shell's current transition window and less central to the selected paper logic.
- Chevron 2013 is far enough back for the main set because it provides a pre-Wirth incumbent baseline while staying connected to the capital-discipline, portfolio-renewal, and shale-era logic that carries into 2018, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024.
- Chevron 2025 is documented as a future follow-up candidate, not a selected year, because the official Chevron annual-report PDF returned HTTP 503 during the latest audit. The available SEC filing is useful context but is not a substitute for the official stockholder-letter PDF in the selected corpus.
