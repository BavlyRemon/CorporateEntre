# NVIDIA Selection Rationale

Final selected years: 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2025.

The NVIDIA set now contains seven report-years/letters. It uses official NVIDIA investor-relations annual report / annual review PDFs for 2011-2025, plus the standalone 2025 Jensen Huang CEO letter supplied from NVIDIA's official image CDN. The selected years privilege documents where the extracted text contains a clear letter address and Jensen Huang CEO/founder signature. Under this stricter rule, 2016 is retained as background because it has a "Dear NVIDIANs and Stakeholders" section but not a clean Jensen Huang signature on the letter section.

## Selected Years

### 2017 - AI platform expansion

2017 is selected as the earliest clean NVIDIA CEO-letter source in the final set. The extracted text contains a "DEAR NVIDIANS AND STAKEHOLDERS" address and a Jensen Huang, CEO and Founder signature. Strategically, it captures GPU computing and AI platform expansion while giving the corpus a signed early-stage CEO text. Source: NVIDIA 2017 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2018 - Volatility and portfolio breadth

2018 is selected because it captures NVIDIA before the later generative-AI surge, when gaming, crypto-cycle volatility, automotive, data center, and AI breadth were all part of the narrative. It is useful for ambidexterity because the text can show how NVIDIA balanced core markets, emerging AI options, and market-cycle turbulence. Source: NVIDIA 2018 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2020 - Data-center expansion and platform renewal

2020 is selected because it gives a clear strategic-renewal bridge between the earlier AI/deep-learning platform thesis and the later generative-AI acceleration. The extracted text includes Jensen Huang / CEO-founder markers and a "DEAR NVIDIANS" address. The year is especially useful for analyzing data-center expansion, Mellanox-related platform logic, ecosystem breadth, and accelerated computing as a long-term strategic option. Source: NVIDIA 2020 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2021 - Omniverse and platform breadth

2021 is selected because it widens the platform story beyond chips into software, simulation, Omniverse, AI, and ecosystem scaling. It is a strong middle-period text for explore-vs-exploit analysis because NVIDIA was exploiting accelerated-computing momentum while exploring new platform layers. Source: NVIDIA 2021 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2022 - Supply-chain/geopolitical and platform breadth

2022 is selected because it is a clean signed CEO letter and a useful bridge between platform-breadth language and the 2023 generative-AI shock. The extracted text contains a "DEAR NVIDIANS" address and a Jensen Huang, CEO and Founder signature. It adds resilience, developer ecosystem, supply-chain/geopolitical context, and AI-platform transition language. Source: NVIDIA 2022 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2023 - Generative AI takeoff

2023 is selected because it captures the generative-AI demand shock and NVIDIA's full-stack AI positioning. The extracted annual report text contains "Dear NVIDIANs" and Jensen Huang / CEO-founder signature markers, so it is usable as a CEO/stakeholder narrative. This year is analytically central for horizon scanning, ecosystem logic, option scaling, and the shift from exploration to large-scale exploitation of a previously developed technology platform. Source: NVIDIA 2023 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16.

### 2025 - AI infrastructure scale and mature platform renewal

2025 is selected because it has both the official annual report PDF and a standalone NVIDIA CEO-letter PDF. The standalone document is addressed to NVIDIANs and stakeholders and is signed by Jensen Huang as CEO and Founder, making it the cleanest latest NVIDIA letter source in the workspace. Strategically, it captures NVIDIA after the generative-AI takeoff, when the analysis can focus on AI infrastructure scale, AI factory logic, ecosystem orchestration, supply/execution pressure, and mature platform renewal. Sources: NVIDIA 2025 annual report / annual review, official NVIDIA Investor Relations archive, accessed 2026-04-16; NVIDIA 2025 Annual Review / CEO letter, official NVIDIA image CDN, accessed 2026-04-16.

## Why These Seven Work Together

The seven-year set gives NVIDIA enough temporal density to analyze a fast-moving innovation cycle. 2017-2018 capture early signed CEO-letter evidence for AI-platform formation and volatility; 2020-2022 capture data-center/platform expansion, ecosystem breadth, and resilience; 2023-2025 capture generative AI and infrastructure scale. This is a stronger CE design than selecting only famous AI-boom years because it shows the explore-to-exploit pathway before, during, and after the inflection.

## Why Other Candidate Years Were Not Selected

2011-2015 were downloaded and retained because they provide useful baseline context, but the extraction does not cleanly isolate a CEO letter. 2016 has a "Dear NVIDIANs and Stakeholders" section, but the letter section is not cleanly signed by Jensen Huang, so it is retained as background under the stricter CEO-letter rule. 2019 is a useful post-crypto reset year, but 2018 and 2020 together give the cleaner volatility-to-platform-renewal transition. 2024 is a strong AI-scaling year and remains useful for background, but 2025 is preferred because it has the cleaner standalone CEO-letter source and a more current post-inflection strategic frame.

## Comparability Note

NVIDIA's years are not forced to match Amazon, Shell, or Chevron exactly. They supply comparable strategic episodes: early platform acceleration, market-cycle turbulence, data-center/platform renewal, ecosystem expansion, demand shock, and mature infrastructure execution. This supports cross-case comparison while respecting NVIDIA's unusually fast AI-cycle timing.
